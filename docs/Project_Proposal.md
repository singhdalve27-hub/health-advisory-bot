## 2. Problem Statement
Accessing straightforward health guidance during localized disease outbreaks in the Philippines often requires sifting through dense medical documents[cite: 4]. The current manual process relies on physical pamphlets or navigating non-interactive static PDFs[cite: 4]. This creates delays in identifying warning signs or executing critical immediate steps, such as proper animal bite cleaning or floodwater exposure management[cite: 4]. 

## 3. Target Users
* **Primary Users:** Community residents, parents, and local health workers seeking fast, plain-language answers regarding common infectious diseases[cite: 4]. 
* **Secondary Users:** Barangay health center volunteers utilizing the assistant as a quick-reference tool for triage and public safety guidance[cite: 4]. 

## 4. Dataset Source(s)
* **Data Type:** PDF[cite: 4]
* **Source:** "7323175-Doh-Communicable-Diseases.pdf" published by the Department of Health, National Center for Disease Prevention and Control[cite: 3, 4].
* **Volume:** 1 PDF document consisting of 80 pages[cite: 3, 4].

## 5. Success Criteria
* **Technical Accuracy:** The RAG system accurately cites specific disease sections (e.g., Dengue, Rabies, Leptospirosis) for 100% of user queries[cite: 4]. 
* **Performance:** System response time remains under 2.5 seconds per query[cite: 4]. 
* **Utility:** Correctly details emergency immediate actions (e.g., 10–15 minute wound washing for animal bites) without generating unverified medical instructions[cite: 4].
